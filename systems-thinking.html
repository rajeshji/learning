Systems Thinking

Interview notes — Senior / Staff Engineer level

Definition

Systems Thinking is analyzing how components interact to produce overall system behavior, rather than optimizing components in isolation.

Feedback Loops

Output of a system becomes its future input.

TypeMeaningExamples
Positive (Reinforcing)Amplifies changeRetry Storm, Viral Growth, Recommendations
Negative (Balancing)Stabilizes systemAuto Scaling, Rate Limiting, Circuit Breaker

Interview tip — always ask: "Will this change amplify or stabilize the system?"

Stocks & Flows

Stock = stored resource  •  Flow = rate at which the stock changes

Emergent Behavior

Unexpected behavior caused by interaction between multiple components.

Retry Storm Cache Stampede Cascading Failure Thundering Herd Autoscaling Thrashing

Healthy components ≠ Healthy system

Interview Q&A

Q1. What is Systems Thinking?
Understanding the interactions between system components to predict overall system behavior.
Q2. What is a Feedback Loop?
A mechanism where a system's output influences its future behavior. It can reinforce growth (positive) or stabilize the system (negative).
Q3. What are Stocks & Flows?
Stocks are stored resources (queues, memory, connections). Flows are the rates that increase or decrease those resources.
Q4. What is Emergent Behavior?
Unexpected system behavior arising from interactions among multiple correctly functioning components.
Q5. Why is Systems Thinking important?
  • Predict cascading failures
  • Identify bottlenecks
  • Improve resiliency
  • Design scalable distributed systems
  • Make better architecture trade-offs

30-Second Revision

Systems Thinking * Optimize the whole system, not individual components. * Feedback Loop: Reinforcing (amplifies) or Balancing (stabilizes). * Stock: Stored resource (queue, memory, connections). * Flow: Rate of change (produce/consume, open/close). * Inflow > Outflow = Bottleneck = Latency = Failure. * Emergent Behavior: Unexpected failures from interactions. * Think in dependencies, feedback, and trade-offs.
